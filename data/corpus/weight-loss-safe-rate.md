## Recommended pace
People who lose weight gradually and steadily, about 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) per week, are more successful at keeping it off. A practical upper bound used in clinical practice is roughly 1% of body weight per week. Faster loss increases loss of lean mass, gallstone risk and the likelihood of regain.

## Size of the deficit
A daily energy deficit of about 500 kcal produces roughly 0.5 kg of weight loss per week; a common approximation is that 1 kg of body fat stores about 7,700 kcal. Deficits are usually capped at 500-750 kcal per day, or about 20-25% of maintenance needs, for unsupervised plans.

## Calorie floors
Diets below about 1,200 kcal per day for women and 1,500 kcal per day for men make it hard to meet nutrient needs and are generally used only under medical supervision. Very-low-calorie diets (about 800 kcal per day or less) require clinical monitoring.
