## No single ideal diet
There is no one ideal percentage of calories from carbohydrate, protein and fat for people with diabetes; eating patterns should be individualised. Mediterranean, DASH, plant-based and lower-carbohydrate patterns all have evidence of benefit.

## Carbohydrate quality
Emphasise non-starchy vegetables, minimal added sugars and refined grains, and whole foods over highly processed foods. Choose carbohydrate from vegetables, pulses, fruit, dairy and whole grains, which are higher in fiber and have a lower glycaemic impact. Replace sugar-sweetened beverages with water. The diabetes plate method fills half the plate with non-starchy vegetables, a quarter with protein and a quarter with carbohydrate foods.

## Weight and insulin
For people with overweight, modest weight loss of 5-7% improves glycaemic control. People using insulin or insulin secretagogues (such as sulfonylureas) can have hypoglycaemia if carbohydrate intake changes, so carbohydrate changes should be coordinated with their care team. Type 1 diabetes needs carbohydrate counting matched to insulin dosing.
