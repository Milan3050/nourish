## Individual targets
People with chronic kidney disease (CKD) should work with a renal dietitian, because needs change with the stage of kidney disease and with dialysis. Limits on protein, sodium, potassium and phosphorus are set individually.

## Protein
Eating more protein than the body needs can make the kidneys work harder. Many people with CKD who are not on dialysis are advised to eat smaller portions of protein foods. People on dialysis often need more protein, not less.

## Minerals
Too much potassium or phosphorus can build up in the blood when kidneys are not working well. High-potassium foods include bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, dried beans and many salt substitutes; phosphorus is high in dairy, processed meats, colas and foods with phosphate additives. High-protein supplements and creatine are generally not recommended without nephrologist approval.
